Small Gods,Large Debts

Every miracle comes due.

The gods are small. Their ambitions aren't. Take on a divine identity, negotiate a Covenant, and borrow power from someone who absolutely intends to collect.

The premise

Power today.
Obligations tomorrow.

Devotion buys miracles. Glory decides who rises. Covenants make promises enforceable—and defaulting can be tempting when the stakes are high enough.

01 / The game

An economy of impossible favors.

Compete as a struggling minor god: accumulate Devotion, chase Glory, and bargain with opponents for the power you can't afford alone. A Miracle may rescue your position today—then leave another deity holding the terms of your tomorrow.

02 / Your decisions

01

Perform miracles

Spend scarce Devotion for supernatural interventions that shift the balance between competing gods.

02

Negotiate sacred contracts

Offer, accept or refuse Covenants. Borrowing a rival's power creates a debt that can shape both players' next decisions.

03

Choose when to pay

Honor an obligation to preserve an alliance—or consider defaulting when the immediate advantage seems worth the consequences.

The strategic hookYour greatest advantage may belong to somebody else.

Financing another god can help an ally, empower an opponent or create leverage for a much more dangerous bargain.

04 / The pantheon

Gods with very human problems.

Our first illustrated playable roster is a proposed six-god design, not a locked set of balanced abilities.

02

Another hour is always possible. Tomorrow is somebody else's problem.

03

Money & favors

A magnificent broker who can put a price on almost anything.

04

The unfinished work that changes shape the moment you look away.

05

Every opinion can be counted. None can be completely controlled.

The wider proposed mythology also includes love, music, the morning after, gossip, chaos and divine ownership. Those characters may appear in card effects and artwork without increasing the six-card Deity inventory.
