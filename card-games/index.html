The collection / Five original games

Five ways to
play differently.

From physically shifting architecture to improbable divine debts and suspicious AI outputs, each title is built around a distinct mechanical idea. These are developing game concepts, not announced retail releases.

01 / Exploration & spatial puzzlesIn development

Not to
Scale

The house is changing. So is the way out.

Build an impossible mansion one room at a time. Each card becomes part of a shifting floor plan—new doors connect, old routes unravel, and familiar spaces refuse to stay where you left them.

Search for evidence and find an exit while the house rewrites its own geometry. The maze is made from the cards in your hands.

Modular roomsShifting routesPhysical exploration

Development focus: intuitive room connections, replayable layouts, and satisfying real-table navigation.

02 / Strategy & negotiationIn development

Small Gods,
Large Debts

Grant miracles. Collect devotion. Pay eventually.

Play as a minor deity with ambitions much larger than your power. Make miracles, offer bargains, secure followers, and sign covenants that can return to haunt you.

Every favor creates leverage. Every promise creates a liability. The question isn't whether the gods will make deals—it's who gets left holding the debt.

Miracles & obligationsDivine economyPolitical bargaining

Development focus: a legible debt economy, meaningful negotiation, and memorable asymmetric decisions.

03 / Persuasion & hidden motivesIn development

The Trial of
a Dead God

The god is dead. The truth is negotiable.

A fallen deity stands at the center of an impossible tribunal. Players interpret competing evidence, challenge testimony, cultivate alliances, and steer the final judgment toward their own interests.

The most convincing story can change the verdict—even when it isn't the whole truth.

Evidence & testimonyHidden agendasCollective judgment

Development focus: dramatic discussion with clear stakes, decisions, and replayability.

04 / AI-assisted social deduction54-card illustrated alpha

Who Broke
the Prompt?

Everyone has instructions. Someone has other plans.

Work together to satisfy a bizarre client's assignment using a shared AI prompt. One player secretly wants the results to fail, and everybody gets to influence the instructions.

Audit the outputs, investigate suspicious contributions, and make your accusation. A wrong answer is evidence—but evidence of what?

Hidden saboteurPrompt manipulationSocial investigation

Development focus: consistent adjudication and fair sabotage opportunities. The illustrated promotional poster shows concept art, not approved print files.

05 / Creative party comedy72-card illustrated alpha

Unsupervised.

Give an AI one job. Then make it everybody's problem.

Players mix absurd scenarios with improbable ideas, while a rotating Operator uses a shared AI chat to turn the combinations into comedy. Each round can leave behind a piece of fictional canon that shapes what happens next.

Unexpected callbacks and escalating nonsense are the point; the people at the table still choose the funniest outcome.

Creative improvisationShared AI chatEvolving canon

Development focus: accessible setup, human-first humor, and a smooth experience on ordinary devices. Promotional card imagery is conceptual, not final manufacturing artwork.

The studio's design philosophy

Mechanics first.
Distinct worlds always.

Original settings, expressive physical components, and systems that make players generate the story together. Five different games; one obsession with what happens when people have competing reasons to act.

01

Meaningful decisions

Deception, negotiation, spatial reasoning, and improvisation have concrete consequences at the table.

02

Designed for real people

Clear turn structures, tactile presentation, and honest access requirements, including the AI games' device dependency.

03

Indie by intention

Each title gets a visual identity of its own: restrained graphic art, tactile textures, and uncluttered typography.
