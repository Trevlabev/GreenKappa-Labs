No Such
Room

The floor plan is lying.

The cards become the playing board. Add square Rooms, walk their passages, and use rotations, exchanges, and Folds to change which parts of the house connect.

Work together to retrieve three architectural Proofs and get every pawn back to the Foyer before the twelfth Shift. Even the staircases and over-under crossings can determine which route exists.

2–4 players45 cards / 19 Rooms2 AP per turnForecasted Shifts

Physical playable-alpha build v0.4.0-P · Ghost Blueprint visual candidate v0.5.0-P · 35–60 minutes targeted, not measured · No release date.

02 / Mythic Negotiation Strategy GameIllustrated concept · Rules untested

Small Gods,
Large Debts

Grant miracles. Collect devotion. Pay eventually.

Play a desperate minor god with an extraordinary gift and an outstanding tab. Spend Devotion on miracles, pursue Glory, and offer binding Covenants to rivals whose help might be expensive.

The concept explores six asymmetric gods—including a deity of fleeting intimacy, a god of benders, an impossible artist, and an artificial oracle—in a fluorescent, abstract celestial economy. Deity names and effects remain provisional.

Miracles & obligationsDivine economyPolitical bargaining

3–6 players proposed · 54-card inventory · 30–45 minutes targeted, not yet playtested. Art and card text are development concepts.

03 / Card-Based Party Game · Interactive ServerPlayable online · Digital product finished

AGENT/FAULT

One agent. Everyone else is a problem.

A Judge privately programs an AI agent for a strange assignment while contestants use Interference cards from private hands. Join the hosted game from your phone, or facilitate a physical prototype with a shared AI chat.

Classic pits independent anonymous AI replies against one another. Cascade instead builds one continuing conversation with a Handoff after somebody else’s card. Human judges award Spotlight and an optional explicit Breakpoint/Fault Bonus.

66 illustrated cardsHosted multiplayerClassic / Cascade

Finished digital product with two live browser editions; physical printing is not certified. Balance and human playtest validation remain separate.

The studio's design philosophy

Give players a reason
to make trouble.

The interesting part often starts after someone makes a bad decision and has to defend it. Each game builds a different kind of consequence into its rules.

01

Decisions with consequences

Players can bluff, bargain, explore, or improvise—and then deal with what follows.

02

A table comes first

Cards should be readable and turns easy to follow. AGENT/FAULT needs a shared chat; the other games do not.

03

Each game has its own look

Illustration, typography, and physical components are being designed for the needs of each game, not a single house style.
