01 / Exploration & spatial puzzlesIn development

No Such
Room

The floor plan is lying.

Build an impossible house from square Room cards. Rotate, swap, and fold the connected structure while planning around visible Shift Forecasts.

Recover three distinct Proofs and escape through the Foyer before the final Shift. The cards are not a map of the house; they are its rooms.

Modular roomsShifting routesPhysical exploration

Next test: whether physical door connections and shifting rooms stay readable as the layout grows.

The god is dead. The truth is negotiable.

04 / AI-assisted social deduction54-card illustrated alpha

Who Broke
the Prompt?

Everyone has instructions. Someone has other plans.

Work together to satisfy a bizarre client's assignment using a shared AI prompt. One player secretly wants the results to fail, and everybody gets to influence the instructions.

Examine the AI's answer, question the selected instructions, and work out who had a reason to wreck the brief. A bad result isn't proof by itself.

Hidden saboteurPrompt manipulationSocial investigation

Next test: fair sabotage at different player counts, with human scoring that doesn't confuse model unpredictability with intent. Promotional art is not print-approved.

05 / AI party sabotagev0.4-P concept · Unplaytested

AGENT/FAULT

One agent. Everyone else is a problem.

The Judge privately programs an AI agent to handle an absurd public assignment. Other players secretly submit reusable Interference cards to push it toward surprising mistakes.

Two public Breakpoints, a hidden universal Fault and the Judge's fallback comedy vote determine the winning response. One shared text-AI chat is enough.

Secret agent profileBlind interferenceHuman judging

Proposed 72-card edition; a 44-card mechanical test is planned. Gameplay, artwork and balance are not final or physically tested.

The studio's design philosophy

Give players a reason
to make trouble.

The interesting part often starts after someone makes a bad decision and has to defend it. Each game builds a different kind of consequence into its rules.

01

Decisions with consequences

Players can bluff, bargain, explore, or improvise—and then deal with what follows.

02

A table comes first

Cards should be readable and turns easy to follow. The two AI-assisted games need a shared chat; the others do not.

03

Each game has its own look

Illustration, typography, and physical components are being designed for the needs of each game, not a single house style.
