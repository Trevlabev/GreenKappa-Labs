Design note / 15 November 2026

Why we started the Playground with a moving house

The interesting part of No Such Room isn't a complicated floor plan. It's what happens when a room moves and takes you with it. That's the one idea the first Playground experiment needed to test.

We made every room visible from the start. The browser version keeps two actions per turn, forecasted shifts, moving rooms, and three records to recover. It leaves out the exploration deck, matching doors, rotation, tools, and Echo. Those still belong to the physical prototype.

The browser experiment can show whether moving rooms is enjoyable. It cannot tell us whether placing physical cards or matching doors works at a table. Both need their own tests, and we haven't completed those human playtests yet.

Before the next revision

What needs testing

  • Can a new player finish a turn without someone explaining the controls?
  • Can players read the action counter and next Shift on a phone?
  • Do predicted room swaps change decisions, or just interrupt them?
  • Does the physical version work with real cards, door connections, and a limited table?

No playtest outcomes or audience metrics are claimed here.
