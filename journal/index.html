Design note / 15 November 2026

Why the first GreenKappa Playground experiment is a shifting house

A good prototype should isolate the mechanical idea worth testing. For Not to Scale, that idea is simple and unsettling: the room is the object, and the person standing inside travels with it when the house rearranges.

The browser microgame starts with nine visible rooms. It keeps the action budget, visible future shifts, movable rooms, and three-record escape goal. It deliberately excludes the tabletop game's exploration deck, door-matching constraints, physical room rotation, tools and Echo rules.

That separation matters. A demo can introduce the feeling of the game without pretending to validate the full design. Next steps are observed playtests of the physical v0.3 rules, alongside a separate usability review of this browser experiment.

Current research questions

What would convince us?

  • New players can learn the prototype without a facilitator.
  • Actions and forecasts remain readable on mobile.
  • A shift creates a meaningful choice rather than random frustration.
  • The printed game works with its actual component constraints.

No playtest outcomes or audience metrics are claimed here.
