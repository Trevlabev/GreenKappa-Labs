# GreenKappa Labs — Editorial Voice Standard
Approved direction: Moderate Countervoice revision · 15 November 2026

## Premise
Describe what a product does and what the visitor can do with it before promising a philosophy. Keep the approved studio tagline **Strange ideas. Serious systems.** as a short anchor, not a phrase to paraphrase in every section.

## Editorial registers
- **Games:** Precise mechanics, dry humor, distinctive stakes. Let each game's writing reflect its decisions: disorientation in *Not to Scale*, obligations in *Small Gods, Large Debts*, rhetoric in *The Trial of a Dead God*, suspicious instructions in *Who Broke the Prompt?*, and blind agent interference in *AGENT/FAULT*.
- **TableArc:** Technical but accessible. Explain canonical state, runtime responsibility, persistence, rule resolution, and provider options only where relevant. Distinguish tested behavior from intended functionality.
- **Playground:** Show the playable experiment first. Keep instructions and rule differences clear.
- **Lab Notes:** Identify decisions, limitations, and unresolved questions. No invented user reactions, usage statistics, or personal anecdotes.
- **Tools and Work:** Lead with artifacts, observed behavior, and verifiable scope. Omit claims of excellence unless demonstrated.

## Avoid patterns that flatten the writing
1. Serial 'not X, but Y' corrections; use this construction only when a distinction is necessary for the reader.
2. Repeated 'three-beat' slogans, generic manifesto headings, and one-line summary echoes.
3. Adjectives without examples: meaningful, innovative, compelling, exceptional, powerful, dynamic, or sophisticated.
4. Empty professional phrases: 'intersection of', 'robust solutions', 'creative ecosystem', 'make complexity legible'.
5. Identical product paragraphs and formulaic 'Development focus' endings. Keep status consistent, but vary explanation by mechanic.
6. Repeated declarations of authenticity or art originality when a simple, verifiable description would suffice.
7. Statements that imply user testing, production readiness, licensing, sales, or public availability without evidence.

## Preserve deliberately
- Proper names, exact card counts, game rules, dates, links, status qualifications, technical vocabulary, and accessibility instructions.
- Established game taglines and purposeful jokes. A distinctive sentence can be symmetrical or use an em dash without being a problem.
- The difference between *prototype*, *illustrated alpha*, *print concept*, and *manufacturing-approved release*.
- The distinction between original commercial work and unofficial fan creations.
- Existing data attributes, IDs, CSS hooks, links, ARIA labels, JSON-LD, and JavaScript interactions during purely editorial changes.

## Publish checklist
- Compare the complete source and revision for changed claims and hidden numerical drift.
- Confirm HTML structure, link targets, and action wording.
- Read neighboring sections together to identify repeated rhetorical structures.
- Verify GitHub Pages deployment independently of GitHub repository commits.
- Assess the writing by precision, voice, and usability—not an invented AI-authorship percentage.
